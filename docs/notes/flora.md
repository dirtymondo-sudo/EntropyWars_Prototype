## 00. THE SCENIC PASS: composing, not scattering (2026-11-25, READ FIRST)

mondo, 11-25, on the Woods' before / after: "i still feel like all of these plants are just randomly placed and it looks ugly as hell.
you need to do a beauty/scenic pass on all the zones now. Thats what i really meant." Section 0 made the GROUND (grass, tufts,
pebbles) less samey. This section is about the PLACEMENT of everything bigger than a tuft: trees, shrubs, ferns, flowers, hero plants,
rocks, logs. What his picture shows, and what every zone must stop doing:
- single plants of many different kinds dotted at even spacing over an open lawn, none touching anything;
- hero pieces (a giant lily, a thorn bush, a big fern) standing alone in the open with nothing round them;
- no grouping, no layers, no edge, no place for the eye to land: the same density everywhere, so nothing reads as a place.

### 00b. The scenic rules (S1-S12)

**S1 · ONE FOCAL A SPACE.** Every space the walker stands in (a clearing, a square, a garden, a bend of the trail) has ONE thing to look at:
a hero tree, a rock outcrop, a ruin, a pond, a statue, a building's door. It stands in the MIDGROUND of the main way in (10-30 m from
where you enter), and it is the place of most contrast (the lightest bloom, the darkest trunk, the only red).

**S2 · FRAME IT.** Taller things (trees, big shrubs, a wall) stand left and right of the way in and at a path's bends, so the view
opens like a window onto the focal. Nothing tall stands between the entrance and the focal.

**S3 · THREE TIERS (Oudolf).** MATRIX = the rule's grass and ground cover, continuous, 60-70 % of the green. PRIMARY = 2 or 3 species
per zone (its palette), in drifts and clumps, 20-30 %. SCATTER = a few accents, 5-10 %, the same few repeated for rhythm. Never one each
of many species.

**S4 · GROUPS, NOT SINGLES.** Plants stand in groups of 3, 5 or 7, mixed sizes (one large, two middle, the rest small), on a loose
triangle, never in a line, a ring or a grid. A drift is long and narrow, follows the ground (a bank, a path, a wall), and its ends thin
out. A lone plant only as a deliberate accent, and never more than one in view.

**S5 · ANCHOR EVERY GROUP.** A group grows FROM something: a trunk's foot, a rock, a log, a wall corner, a step, a path's bend, a
water's edge. Nothing floats in the middle of an open lawn.

**S6 · LAYER THE EDGES.** Where wood meets open ground: grass, then a fringe of tall grass and flowers (1-2 m), then shrubs and ferns
(2-4 m), then small trees, then the canopy. Feathered: the bands interlock, the line wanders. A path gets a soft band of low plants
on both sides, denser on the outside of a bend.

**S7 · KEEP THE OPEN OPEN.** A clearing, a lawn, a square is NEGATIVE SPACE: matrix grass only in its middle, the planting at its rim.
Density rises from the middle to the edges, never the same everywhere.

**S8 · NO HERO ALONE.** A big or strange piece (a giant flower, a thorn bush, a mushroom, a fae cap, a statue) always stands with its
supporting layer (low plants at its foot, a smaller one of its kind nearby) and against a backdrop (a trunk, a rock, a wall), never in the open.

**S9 · VIGNETTES ON THE WAY.** Every 20-40 m of path, a small composed scene that tells something: a fallen log with ferns and mushrooms,
a rock with moss and a puddle, a bench under a tree with a drift of flowers, a shrine with offerings and blossom, a broken cart in tall
grass. Built from the zone's own pieces, at a path's side or bend, framed and seen from the path.

**S10 · LEAD THE EYE, HIDE AND REVEAL.** Paths curve; rows of trees, a hedge, a stream, a fence run toward the focal or the landmark.
The landmark is hidden by planting along the way and revealed at a bend (the weenie).

**S11 · A PALETTE PER ZONE.** Each zone's primary species and accent colour are few and repeated (section 5's climates name them). The
accent colour sits at the focal. Variety comes from size, age and grouping, not from more species.

**S12 · WHO DOES WHAT.** The RULE (flora field, grass, pebbles) does the matrix and the texture, and keeps out of a composed space's middle.
A ZONE'S THREAD composes the structure by hand: focal, framing, edges, groups, vignettes, the landmark's reveal, in its district files
with the shared kit below. Even scatter rows of single plants (the old "throw N props over the district" rows) are replaced by groups.

### 00c. How a zone's thread does its pass (the checklist)

1. Walk the room in the probe; for each space write down: its way in, its focal, what frames it, where its open middle is.
2. Delete or move every single plant dotted over open ground (S4, S5, S7); move hero pieces onto anchors with their supporting layer (S8).
3. Plant the edges in bands (S6) and the groups at anchors (S4, S5) from the zone's palette (S3, S11).
4. Add a vignette every 20-40 m of path (S9) and the landmark's reveal (S10).
5. Before / after pictures of the same views from the walker's camera; post them.
6. `node find-softlocks.js <room>` and the door finder if anything solid moved; nothing blocks a path or a door.

**THE GROUPS KIT (11-25, the Woods pilot; use it in every zone).** data.js `ZONE_FLORA.compose[<climate>]` + `zoneFloraCompose`, run by
hqTerrainCompile right after the scatter loop (room build, never a frame). A climate's row names its plant keys: `{ thin, loose, low, keys: { <key>:
{ per: [min, max], size: [3, 5, 7], on: ['tree', 'edge', 'log', 'stump', 'rock', 'wall'], low } } }`. Those keys' scatter rows stop sprinkling, and the
room's hand rows of those keys standing more than `loose` m from any anchor are dropped where they stood (`info.composeDrop`, read by zoneFloraSwap).
The same plants (times `thin`) then stand as GROUPS: a hero by an anchor on its open side, the rest of the key and the `low` layer round it on a loose
triangle, mixed sizes. Anchors: `info.trees`, the thicket's front rows (`edge`), and scatter / prop rows matched by `ZONE_FLORA.anchorKeys` (logs,
stumps, rocks), building corners (`wall`). Seeded by the room (both players see the same). Off: `EW_NO_FLORA_COMPOSE`. Readout: `hqTerrainInfo(room).composed`.
S7 in the flora field: set `near: true, far: 0..0.05` on a climate's flower / fern layers (the Woods do it from their own block) and the open middle
stays grass. A new zone = one `compose` row + the layer flags; no room file edited.
**Round 2 (11-25, mondo: "the whole point ... was to break up the grass ... with rocks and plants and flowers ... the plants ... dont have any grassblades and
are just on a flat field").** So S7 is softened in the Woods: (1) a row's `islands` puts a half-sunk rock or two with plants at its foot out in the open grass,
one per `every` m2 (the Clearing 24, the Ritual Ground 27); (2) the flower / fern layers keep `far` 0.55 on grass (drifts in the grass), 0.05 on bare dirt;
(3) the forest's bare dirt (dirt_2, no blades) gets the dark kit tufts as a non-grass layer, so a group stands IN growth; (4) `grassH` 0.6 for the Woods.
Probe lesson: the blade grass streams in over a few seconds; a view needs `"settle": 9000` or the picture shows bare sheet where the game has grass.

### 0b. The rules

**G1 · CLUMPS, NOT A CARPET.** Grass grows in clumps 1.5-4 m across. Each clump gets its own height (x0.6 to x1.5 of the base),
its own tint (a hue / brightness step) and its own lean. Between clumps the cover thins. A field never shows the jittered grid.

**G2 · THREE SCALES OF COLOUR.** (a) per blade: dark foot to light tip, about one blade in ten dry straw; (b) per clump: a tint
step; (c) per patch (15-40 m): the whole ground drifts between lush green, yellow-green and olive / dry, and the dry share rises
to a third in the dry patches. A sheet's own colour stays the anchor (never repaint his sheets); the tint rides on top.

**G3 · BARE AND THIN PATCHES.** 8-15 % of open grass is thin or bare (the sheet shows through), in soft-edged blobs a few
metres across. Grass is shortest and thinnest on and beside paths (trampled), on rough / rocky ground and under a dense canopy.

**G4 · THE EDGES ARE TALL.** Grass and weeds stand TALLER where nothing walks or mows: the foot of a wall, fence, rock, building
or trunk, the field's rim, the bank of water. A tall fringe (x1.3-1.8) a metre or so wide, with a weed or a flower in it.

**G5 · PLANTS HAVE A REASON TO BE THERE.** Every layer reads the spot (the Horizon / Far Cry rule):
- ferns, saplings and low bushes: at tree bases, by logs and rocks, in shade; groups of 3-7; never sprinkled over open lawn;
- flowers: in DRIFTS of one or two species (2-6 m), with grass between drifts; never an even confetti of mixed colours;
- reeds and rushes: only within 2-3 m of fresh water;
- dry weeds, sage, pebbles: on rough, rocky and dry ground;
- leaf litter and twigs: under deciduous trees; needles under firs.

**G6 · BREAK IT WITH STONE.** Every green zone gets ground SCATTER that is not a plant: pebbles and small stones (walk-through,
ankle-high), a few larger rocks half-sunk in the ground (the existing `rf_rock_*` / rock models, placed by a holder: they block),
in clusters of 2-5 of mixed sizes, more on rough ground and slopes, near paths and at a rock's foot. Never a lone, evenly spaced rock.

**G7 · A GROUND PLANT MUST READ AS A PLANT.** No single flat card that shows as a sheet from the walker's camera or from above;
no plant floating, sunk to its leaves or at a wrong scale (a fern is knee-high, 0.4-0.9 m; a flower 0.15-0.5 m). If a plant model
looks bad from the game camera, replace it with a better existing one or drop it from the list. Never let one plant model carpet
a whole room: no single model above about 40 % of a layer.

**G8 · THE RIGHT TREES.** A zone plants only its climate's trees (section 5): no palm outside the tropics, the coast and the
city's sea front; no snow tree off snow; no birch in the desert. Mixed ages: saplings and old trees, not one size. Trees in
groves with clearings, not a lattice. Trees still only on grass, dirt or sand (owner-rules).

**G9 · A TREE IN PAVEMENT STANDS IN A PIT.** A tree on a city street, plaza or forecourt never stands on one bare sand square that
fades into the concrete. It gets a holder: a raised kerb ring or a square planter (15-45 cm, stone or concrete, reusing the game's
planter / kerb models first), or a flush grate, with soil, mulch or gravel inside (a palm may keep sand, edged). Pit at least
one full square. The same for every zone with paved squares (the Vatican, Katana's town, Camelot's plaza).

**G10 · A SET PIECE IS DESIGNED.** A flower field, a garden, a meadow clearing is a place a zone's thread lays out on purpose: a
shape with soft edges fading over 3-5 m into the surrounding cover, drifts inside it, a path or two through it, a few rocks
breaking it. The rule's base cover never replaces that.

**G11 · PERFORMANCE (RULE #1e).** All of it is decided per cell when a cell is built, from the cell's own lists (the noise, the
room's paths, boxes and fluid read once): never a per-frame scan. Same seeded hash for both players. Every mesh made is disposed
when its cell drops.

**G12 · THE COVER SPLIT.** The ground is ranked by one big, ragged, domain-warped noise (11 m patches, edges pushed about 6 m), so
the split holds in every room: about a QUARTER thin (42 % of tufts kept, under half height: on a grass sheet a bare patch reads as
short lawn, never a flat floor), a QUARTER short sward (0.7 height), HALF dense. Large irregular patches, never even clumps.
Replaces G3's bare blobs (EW_NO_COVER = G3 back). On a dirt sheet the bare quarter shows the dirt, its pebbles and its litter.

**G13 · THE ECOLOGY.** The rank is pushed: UP at a trunk's foot (6.5 m), at the wood's rim (the thicket's mass within 3.5 m) and at
a stump, log or rock (2 m); DOWN within 2.6 m of a path's edge and round a landmark (a grand stair's foot and the 7 m approach in
front of it, 8 m; the room's spawn, 3.5 m). So clearings open round where you walk and arrive, and the cover gathers where nobody does.

**G14 · SIX SILHOUETTES, NO NEW TRIANGLES.** Each tuft is one of six shapes cut from the same fan: fine, arched (tips droop),
leaning (the whole tuft to one side), sparse (half its blades), mat (low and splayed), tuft. Blades are thinner (0.56-0.85 of the
old width), each blade its own height (0.4-1.1), a dropped blade folds to its root. The short sward picks the low shapes.

**G15 · LIGHT IN THE COLOUR.** Per tuft (no new material): cooler and darker under a crown and at the rim, warm yellow-green out in
the open, duller brown-green beside a path, and each clump its own hue step toward yellow-green or blue-green.

**G16 · GROUNDING.** A blade's root sinks 7 % of its height into the ground; its lower half darkens to 0.66 of the root colour.

**G17 · THE WALKER.** The grass bows down and away round the walker's feet (0.7-2 m, shader, WebGL + the WebGPU twin), so he
reads in dense cover. No scan: the walker's spot is one uniform.

Not done yet (asked, waiting): G18 lighting and depth (dappled light, contact shadows; a distance desaturation without fog needs
mondo's OK, bloom + fog stay off); G19 the leaf litter, moss, twigs and roots layer (ground cards / sheets, no tall meshes).

## 1. The one idea: a RULE, not rows

Nothing of this pass is written into a room. A room's foliage is WORKED OUT each time the room is built, from three things
the map already says:

1. **the room's zone** (`data.js ZONE_FLORA.zones`: one row per zone, its rooms and its climate);
2. **the One World map's ground under each spot** (`hqOwGround`: forest, meadow, marsh, tundra, desert ...), for the zones
   whose row says `map: true`;
3. **the ground sheet painted under each plant** (`ZONE_FLORA.sheets`: grass = `lawn`, `grass_dark_fantasy` = forest `floor`,
   `desert` = `sand`, `ice` / `calm_snow` = `snow` ...).

So a zone that is re-laid, grown, split into more rooms, moved on the map or rebaked by its tool keeps its climate's plants,
and no bake tool, district file or layout was edited for this pass. A district thread that plants its usual `tree` rows and
paints its usual sheets comes out in the climate's trees and flowers by itself. The same idea as `ZONE_LOOKS`.

- **A new, renamed or split room** of a zone needs NOTHING: a room with no row of its own takes the row of the zone it belongs to
  on the map (`HQ_ZONE_MAP.zones`, rewritten by `node bake-map.js`; `ZONE_FLORA.mapZones` = map zone -> zone row). This was
  needed the same day: while this pass was being built, Katana Island and the Mountains both changed their room ids on main.
  Give a room its own row only when it differs from its zone (the Fairy Forest, the Snowy Pines, the Redwood Trail, Stonehenge).
- **A room that is in no zone of the map** grows nothing and its trees are untouched (every indoor room, the hub, the old handmade sites).
- Only a SURFACE room falls back to its zone (the Cavern's row also underground): a new cellar under the Beach grows nothing.
- **A sheet with no row** in `ZONE_FLORA.sheets` grows nothing (paving, road, cave floor, cloud, metal).

## 2. The two halves

**THE TREES** (`data.js zoneFloraTrees`, run once by `hqTerrainCompile`). A tree of a PLAIN kind may become its climate's kit
tree. Plain kinds, by class (`ZONE_FLORA.classes`): birch (`tree`, `tree_4`, `tree_53`, `tree_54`), oak (`tree_3`, `tree_52`),
spruce (`tree_2`, `tree_55`), pine (`pine`, `tree_56`), bare (`tree_5`, `tree_6`), snow pine (`xmas_*`). Named trees never
change: palm, cherry, maple, saguaro, the fae trees, the chess pieces, the old OBJ kinds.
- A climate's `trees` list says what each class may become and how often it stays as planted (`'='`).
- **By the water:** a tree within 5 m of open water takes the climate's `water` list (the willows).
- **On snow:** a tree whose own ground sheet is snow takes the winter list (bare birch and oak, snow firs), so the snow line a
  thread paints is also the trees' snow line.
- **The mass** (a room's thicket: thousands of trunks nobody walks among) changes only in its front rows and only to the kit's
  five light trees (`ZONE_FLORA.mass`), because a kit tree is 2 to 5 times the triangles of a painted tree.
- The pick is a hash of the tree's own place and the room's id: the same wood on every visit and for both players online
  (RULE #2). A tree that keeps its kind is exactly as it was. `t.kind0` remembers what was planted.

**THE FLORA FIELD** (`three-renderer.js _hqFloraTick`). The small plants (flowers, ferns, grass tufts, reeds, low bushes)
grow round the walker the way the grass field does: the ground in 14 m cells, each cell ONE merged mesh on one sheet, built
when he comes within 46 m and dropped when he leaves. Per spot: climate (zone + map) -> ground class (sheet; water within
2.2 m = `wet`) -> the climate's layers for that class, thrown by a seeded hash. Never on a path, in water, on a steep face,
under a building or in the thicket. No prop rows, no blockers, nothing a fight reads, nothing relayed (it is the same on
both screens by its seed). A room that grows the blade grass (`terrain.grassField`) gets no kit tufts on top of it.

Off switches (console, before the room is entered): `EW_NO_FLORA` (all), `EW_NO_FLORA_TREES`, `EW_NO_FLORA_FIELD`.
Readout: `window._ewHqFlora` = `{ room, cells, plants, tris, reach, low }`.
**THE ONE KNOB:** `ZONE_FLORA.lush` (2.2) multiplies every flower / fern layer of every climate; `lushG` (1.2) the tufts.
Settings > Video > Grass on low or off, or `EW_PERF_LOW` = reach 30 m and 60 % of the plants.

## 7. Changing it

- A zone should feel different: change its `c`, or its row in §5's table in `ZONE_FLORA.climates`. Nothing else.
- Thicker / thinner everywhere: `ZONE_FLORA.lush`. One climate: its layers' second number.
- A new plant: add it to `bake-foliage.py` (it joins the field file), `--put` the field file, give it a place in a list `M`.
- A new tree kind in a climate: a row in the `T` lists. A kit tree is heavy: only the five in `mass.light` go in a thicket.
- After any change to the field file: its `ASSET_MANIFEST.json` row (`npm run manifest -- <mirror> --merge`), or players keep the old one.
- The tutorial, fights, saves and the online relay do not read any of this.
