Entropy Wars · the world, redrawn

One World

One continent on a flat disc, inside the Ice Wall. The zones no longer touch like tiles: each sits in its own country, with open land, rivers and hills between them, and a road or trail through that country from one to the next. Disaster City is now one city on a big map, not a third of it. Underneath, every hidden way down ends in the same hollow. Out at sea, one voyage circles the land and stops at an island in every direction.

What makes it hold together

  1. Zones sit apart, routes join them. Between any two neighbours there is a stretch of open country, and a route runs through it: a road, a trail, a pass, a causeway. You walk from one place to the next, like a real journey.

  2. A route is narrow; the country is wide. Each route is a walkable band 2 plots wide. The land on either side is seen, not walked: fields, forest, hills and desert that fill the view without needing content.

  3. Every route has something on it. A bridge, an inn, a gas station, a crop circle, a frozen lake, plus wild creatures to fight and capture. No stretch is empty, and each one hides the next zone's loading behind a bend, a tunnel or a ridge.

  4. Everything sits on the grid. The map is one plot grid, centred on Disaster City's south-west corner. Its radius is 44 plots: 528 battle squares, about 1.85 km. Every zone, island and route covers whole plots, and the coast follows plot edges. The numbers round the sheets are battle squares from the centre.

  5. Water runs downhill, land changes gradually. Snow in the north, then foothills, the western forest, farmland, scrub and desert in the south. Three rivers run from the snow to a lake or the sea.

  6. Every horizon tells the truth, and below sits under what it serves. What you see past an edge is the real neighbour; the Sewers are under the city, the D.U.M.B. under Area 51, Hell under the Vatican, Atlantis under the Triangle.

The lay of the land

  • The North Pole is the top of the world, at the top centre of the disc, straight across from Antarctica at the bottom: the village on its cape with the Frozen Sea round it, the Polar Trail climbing to it from the Snowy Pass. Below it the Mountains run across the north. Mount Olympus stands above the Vatican, with Heaven's clouds on the summit.
  • The Vatican and the Colosseum sit in the foothills on the Tiber, a river walk from the city: Rome and the Vatican side by side, the Pilgrim Way climbing to Olympus behind them.
  • The west is the old forest country. Camelot is far out on the west coast, a day's road from the Vatican, with its bay looking out to Avalon. The Woods sit between, in the trees.
  • The middle is farmland and a salt lake, where the Tiber ends. The Estate stands among the fields, with its Ranch on the road south.
  • Disaster City holds the east coast, with the Hills and the sign behind it and the Beach in front.
  • The south is badlands, then the Desert. The highway runs down through gas stations and motels to Exit 1. Giza sits on the Nile, and across the Delta Causeway lies Leylines, on its own peninsula, with the ley line running from the Great Pyramid to Stonehenge.
  • Antarctica is the rim, reached by sea.

Cities on the map

With the land spread out, the cities read as separate places a journey apart: Disaster City, Vatican City and Rome, Camelot, Western Town, the North Pole village, Babel and Technoticlan. Underground: Agartha and Atlantis. Above: Heaven's City.

What you see from where

  • City rooftops: the range and the sign to the north, the Vatican's dome and the Colosseum small in the north-west foothills, the badlands going south.
  • The Vatican Square: Olympus right overhead, the river, the city's towers far off down the valley.
  • Anywhere north: the Pole's cape at the top of the world, white against the Wall.
  • The Estate's fields: the lake, the farms, the forest line to the north.
  • The Desert: the pyramids and the ley line's glow to the south-east.

The Desert, spread out

The Desert's four districts no longer sit in one square. The Circus (3 × 3) is first, where the Badlands Highway arrives from the city. Western Town (4 × 4) is out west, where the Ranch Road comes in. Area 51 (4 × 4) is alone in the deep south, with the D.U.M.B. under it. Giza (4 × 4) is south-east on the Nile, the Coast Road and the Delta Causeway at its door.

The Desert Highway joins all four in a loop. It is built from the Desert room's own Highway plots (district R, 87 today): the loop takes 82, and the other 5 stay as roadside stops (the gas station, the trailers, the Door). So the spread costs no new plots.

The Seas

One sea route, the Round Voyage, circles the continent and stops at a port or island in every direction. You sail the open sea rather than walk it, and each leg is a short boat ride that covers the load. The Beach keeps its own calm near sea off the city.

One hollow, many ways down

Stacked by depth: man-made places shallow, military works deeper, the natural caves deeper still, Hell at the bottom. Hollow Earth sits under the middle of the continent, lit by its inner sun, and the hidden ways down from all over the surface (the Lemuria Stair from Shasta, the Estate wells, the Sewers' Old Workings, the D.U.M.B.'s Level −6, the Hell fissure, the Polar Opening, the way to Technoticlan) all lead to it. With the land spread out these are long passages now, which suits a secret way across the world.

Layers

  • −1 man-made: the Sewers, the Catacombs, the robbers' tunnel under the Delta.
  • −2 deep works: the D.U.M.B. and CERN's ring under Area 51, joined to the Sewers by the Maglev.
  • −3 / −4 the hollow: the Cavern, then Hollow Earth and Agartha.
  • −5 the pit: Hell, under the Vatican, by the fissure.
  • Under the sea: The Deep, Atlantis under the Triangle.

Every one of these ways is in the game already except the Maglev.
